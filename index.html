Three sources of retirement income

Three sources, three organizations

Your retirement income comes from three places, and each one is run by a different organization. Each is the source of truth for its own numbers.

VRS: a pension for life

A monthly pension for the rest of your life, plus the $1,488 Hazardous Duty Supplement until 67.

myvrs.varetire.org

CBIZ: one permanent choice

The County Supplemental Pension Plan, which you and the County both pay into. At retirement you choose once. For most career members that means 180 monthly payments or one lump sum.

myretirement.cbiz.com or 1-844-581-2871

Empower: accounts you own

Your 457(b), Roth 457(b) and 401(a). They hold what you put in, plus the County's match in the 401(a). They are investment accounts, not a guaranteed check.

participant.empower-retirement.com or 1-800-701-8255

Log in to all three

In each one, check your salary history, your years of service and your beneficiaries. A mistake is easy to fix years before you retire and hard to fix after.

Chapter 1 · Source 1: VRS

How your VRS pension is figured

One formula

Your average final compensation, times your years of service, times 1.85%. That is your pension for a year, for life.

a year is a month.

Every year adds at least this much

At the same AFC, each year of service adds a month, for the rest of your life.

That is the floor. Until you top out, step raises also lift your AFC, which lifts every year you have already earned. After top-out, raises are mostly COLAs.

Short of 25 years costs more

Leave between 50 and 60 with fewer than 25 years and VRS cuts 0.5% for every month you are short. At 20 years, that is 30% less, for life.

Pay tops out at 16 years for Technicians, 18 for Lieutenants, 20 for Captains, 22 for Battalion Chiefs and 24 for Division Chiefs, so most of us have the same AFC at 20 as at 25.

Your pension

a month replaces about of your pay.

Your official number is in the myVRS Benefit Estimator. Survivor options and a PLOP would lower it.

Chapter 2 · Source 1: also VRS

The Hazardous Duty Supplement

It sits on top

With 20 years of hazardous duty service, VRS adds a flat $1,488 a month to your pension. Everyone who qualifies gets the same amount, whatever their salary.

For you, that is about of what VRS pays.

It is a bridge

It starts when your VRS retirement begins, age 50 at the earliest, and carries you to full Social Security age, 67.

Starting at , the bridge runs , about in all.

At 67, it stops

The supplement ends at 67, your full Social Security age, whether or not you have started Social Security.

Claim Social Security as early as 62 and you collect both for a while, but your Social Security check stays permanently smaller.

Chapter 3 · Source 2: CBIZ

Your CBIZ choice

One choice, made once

When you retire from the County Supplemental Pension Plan, you pick one option, and you cannot change it later. Which options you have depends on your age when you leave.

Option 1: a bridge to 50

Only for members who reach 25 years before they turn 50, which usually means hired at 24 or younger. It pays monthly until your VRS pension can begin at 50, then stops.

The plan also has a second formula and a supplement. The base benefit may get yearly increases; the supplement never does. CBIZ will show which applies to you.

Option 2: 180 checks that rise each August

The plan document sets this, not a yearly Board vote: after a year of payments, Option 2 gets a cost-of-living increase each August, tied to inflation with limits based on your hire date. CBIZ's real example: members who started at $640 a month in 2017 and 2018 now receive $799.33, 25% more.

If you die before all 180 are paid, your beneficiary receives the rest.

Option 3: one lump sum

When the lump sum can make sense

If you took the lump sum and invested it instead, it would have to earn about 9% a year, every year for 15 years, to pay you as much as Option 2 with 2% increases. But you might use it to pay off a mortgage, clear credit card debt or cover a child's tuition, and the interest you stop paying can tip the math.

That is a conversation for a financial planner. The IAFF Financial Corporation is a good place to start.

Chapter 4 · Source 3: Empower

Your 457(b), Roth 457(b) and 401(a)

Investment accounts, not a guaranteed check

Your pensions pay a set amount every month. These do not. They are investment accounts that you own: what you put in, plus the County's match in the 401(a), and whatever it earns.

The 401(a): you put in 0.5% of base salary and the County matches it 100%. Joining is a one-time choice made in your first 30 days.

When you retire, you decide what to do with the money. How fast you draw it down decides how long it lasts.

Tax now or tax later

The 457(b) comes out of your pay before taxes, and you pay tax when you withdraw. The Roth 457(b) is taxed today, and comes out tax-free later.

Roth tends to come out ahead when your tax rate now is lower than it will be in retirement. Which fits you depends on your own situation.

When you can reach it

Your 457(b) is open at any age once you leave the County, with no 10% penalty. Your 401(a) opens without the penalty if you leave in or after the year you turn 50, or with 25 years. Roth earnings come out tax-free at 59½ once the account is 5 years old.

How the two can work together

Holding both gives you two pockets: 457(b) money to live on from the day you leave until 59½, and Roth money that comes out tax-free after. It matters most if you retire young.

Your balance as a paycheck

Simple division, before growth or taxes. Move these accounts into an IRA and you lose the early-withdrawal protection: before 59½, IRA withdrawals generally face the 10% penalty.

Chapter 5 · Decision at retirement

The PLOP

Your future checks

The Partial Lump-Sum Option Payment lets you take 1, 2 or 3 years of your pension up front. Each square here is one future monthly check of .

Pulled forward into one payment

It is not extra money. It is your own future checks, paid early. You choose it on your VRS retirement application; it cannot be taken while you are still working.

Every check after is smaller

VRS first figures your full pension. The year you worked to qualify counts toward your service and your AFC like any other year. Then, to pay for the PLOP, VRS lowers your monthly pension permanently. Future cost-of-living increases are figured on the smaller check.

VRS calculates the exact reduction. See it in the myVRS Benefit Estimator before you choose.

Taxes come off the top

Taken in cash, VRS withholds 20% for federal tax and 4% for Virginia before you see a dollar. Rolling it into an IRA or a plan that accepts rollovers avoids the up-front tax.

Do you qualify?

You must work at least one year past the date you first qualify for an unreduced pension: 50 with 25 years, or 60 with 5. Purchased service does not count toward that year.

Chapter 6 · Decision at retirement

Who keeps getting paid if you die first

While you are alive

a month: your VRS pension plus the supplement.

At retirement you choose how your VRS pension is paid. Most members never see the trade-off laid out.

The month after you die

On the Basic Benefit, your spouse gets $0 a month from VRS.

At most, VRS refunds any of your contributions left in your account, once.

What keeps paying

Only some pieces continue. The supplement stops whichever option you choose.

CBIZ Option 2 pays your beneficiary the rest of the 180 payments. Option 3 was already paid to you as one lump sum, so nothing continues. Your Empower accounts pass to the beneficiaries you named there.

Your CBIZ beneficiary is named on the plan's own form, not your VRS form. Still working and die in the line of duty? That beneficiary receives a $169,200 lump sum from the plan.

What the Survivor Option costs

Your own check is lower for life so your survivor can keep one. You pick the share: any whole percentage from 10% to 100%.

How much lower depends on that share, your age and your survivor's age. Run 50%, 75% and 100% side by side in the myVRS Benefit Estimator.

Hard to undo

You can change a Survivor Option only in limited cases, such as your survivor dying first or certain divorces, and only once in your lifetime. If you are married, your spouse signs your retirement application.

VRS offers one-on-one counseling and explains every payout option, if you want to talk it through before you sign.

Chapter 8

Health coverage and Medicare

What covers you between your last shift and 65, two credits toward your premiums, and the Medicare deadline that follows you for life.

Medicare starts at 65, not at retirement

Your active-employee medical and dental end on the last day of the month you separate.

Ways members cover the gap

With 15 or more years of County service, you can continue County group medical in retirement until 65, and dental and vision for life.

COBRA, through P&A Group, generally runs up to 18 months. A spouse's employer plan or a marketplace plan are other routes.

Retiree premiums change every plan year. Ask County HR Benefits what you would pay.

Two credits toward premiums, for life

Both repay premiums you actually pay, including Medicare Part B and supplement plans, and together they never pay more than the premium. Both need 15 years of service and count up to 30.

County rate of $6.50 a year of service applies to retirements on or after July 1, 2024. Apply for the VRS credit with your retirement application; it is not automatic. Confirm both amounts with County HR Benefits.

At 65: your Medicare window

Your window opens 3 months before the month you turn 65 and closes 3 months after. Seven months in all.

County group medical for retirees ends at 65.

The standard Part B premium is $202.90 a month in 2026. Higher incomes pay more.

Medicare.gov and CMS, 2026 figures.

Missing it costs for life

Sign up for Part B late and the premium goes up 10% for every full 12 months you could have had it, for as long as you have Part B.

Coverage from a job you or your spouse still works can pause the clock. Retiree coverage and COBRA do not.

Part D drug coverage has its own late penalty. Details at medicare.gov.

Chapter 9

Protections with early deadlines

Two County benefits cost less, or may only be available, if you act years before you retire. Whether either one fits you is your decision.

Long-term care is expensive

Long-term care is help with daily living, at home, in assisted living or in a nursing home. Health insurance and Medicare generally do not pay for extended stays.

A private nursing home room runs $127,750 a year.

National medians, 2024.

It can drain your savings

County benefits staff have told our members that firefighters save well in our 457s but rarely plan for long-term care.

Change your balance under Use your own numbers.

Premiums depend on your age when you enroll

The County has offered group long-term care coverage through Genworth during open enrollment, a benefit very few employers offer.

Premiums are based on your age when you enroll, so coverage started in your 30s or 40s costs far less than coverage started close to retirement. Ask County HR Benefits whether it is still offered and when you can enroll.

Supplemental life: the 5-year rule

To keep County supplemental life coverage after you retire, at the County rate, it has to have been in place for at least 5 years before you retire.

Members have learned this rule at the County retirement meeting 2 or 3 years out, when it was already too late.

These rules can change. Confirm them with County HR Benefits before your next open enrollment.

Chapter 10

Presumptive law: the clock after you leave

Virginia presumes certain diseases in firefighters come from the job. That protection follows you into retirement if you file in time.

What Virginia presumes

Respiratory disease, and with 5 years of service, hypertension, heart disease, leukemia and eleven listed cancers. The County must overcome the presumption with a preponderance of evidence.

If the County gave you a pre-employment physical, it must have found no sign of the condition. After you file, the County can ask you to see a doctor it chooses, and you can have your own doctor there, at your cost.

Heart, hypertension, respiratory: 5 years

File within 2 years after the diagnosis is communicated to you, or 5 years after your last exposure, whichever comes first.

Your last exposure, in the law's words your “last injurious exposure,” is your last on-the-job exposure to a cause, such as smoke at a fire. It is not always your last shift.

Listed cancers: 10 years

File within 2 years after the diagnosis is communicated to you, or 10 years after your last exposure, whichever comes first.

No cancer claim at 65 or older

Whatever the 10-year clock says, a cancer claim cannot be made at age 65 or older.

Whichever comes first

File exposure reports throughout your career and document your last known exposure, because the 5- or 10-year window counts from that date. Request your Occupational Health records while you are still employed.

Diagnosed after you retire? Email the Executive Board at eboard@Local2598.org right away. The Local can connect you with attorneys who handle these claims.

Va. Code § 65.2-402 and § 65.2-406. Line of Duty Act questions go to VRS at 1-888-827-3847. This chart assumes your last exposure is your last shift.

Your last 90 days

Pay, leave and paperwork. None of it is hard, but each piece is easy to miss in your final weeks.

Mobius shows your sick leave balance. This stays on your device.

Your sick leave payout

Sick leave is not paid out hour for hour. A formula decides how much you get.

You start with hours.

The first hours are not paid

On 112 hours, 672 hours come off. On 84 hours, 504. On 80 hours, 480.

Then 25% of what is left

What it might be worth

Annual leave is paid out separately, up to the yearly maximum. Check Mobius to see if you are in the Final Pay Plan. If you are, payable leave rolls tax-deferred into your 457(b), then your 401(a), so do not close those accounts until it lands.

Estimated at your AFC divided by your schedule's yearly hours, before taxes. Payroll sets the real figure.

Your last 120 days

Mail VRS your paper application (VRS-5) 60 to 120 days out. Hazardous duty members cannot apply online, and your spouse signs if you are married.

File your CBIZ application before your last day. In your last month, file an exposure report and request your Occupational Health records.

Medical and dental end on the last day of the month you separate. COBRA paperwork comes from P&A Group, 1-800-688-2611.
